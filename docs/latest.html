With reference to the 'price cap' imposed by G7 countries and allies on Russian seaborne crude oil exports, which one of the following best describes this mechanism?

  1. It imposes additional import tariffs on Russian crude entering G7 territory, raising its landed cost for domestic refiners
  2. It prohibits Western shipping, insurance and financial services from being used to transport Russian oil sold above a specified price, without directly banning its purchase by third countries
  3. It is a WTO-administered mechanism that caps the annual volume of crude oil Russia may export to non-G7 countries
  4. It is a blanket European Union embargo banning all imports of Russian crude oil irrespective of price
Answer

(b) The G7/EU price cap (effective since December 2022) denies Western maritime services — shipping, insurance, financing — to any Russian crude cargo sold above the capped price, leveraging the West's dominance in tanker insurance rather than imposing a direct import ban; this has let third countries like India keep buying, with the narrowing discount reported by BPCL reflecting reduced sanctions-driven price leverage and higher freight costs via a 'shadow fleet'. It is not a tariff, a WTO volume quota, or a blanket EU embargo (the EU's separate import ban is distinct from the price cap mechanism).
