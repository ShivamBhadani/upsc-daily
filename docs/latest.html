Q11 · Science & Technology · Medium

With reference to atmospheric science, which one of the following best describes 'photochemical smog'?

  1. Haze formed when fine particulate matter and soot accumulate in cold, moisture-laden air near the ground, reducing visibility without any chemical transformation of pollutants
  2. An atmospheric phenomenon caused by the direct emission of ground-level ozone from vehicular exhaust and industrial combustion sources
  3. A type of smog formed exclusively through the condensation of water vapour around industrial soot particles in high-humidity, low-temperature conditions typical of winter mornings
  4. A secondary form of air pollution produced when nitrogen oxides and volatile organic compounds react in the presence of sunlight to form ground-level ozone and other oxidants
Answer

(d) Photochemical smog is a secondary pollution phenomenon: nitrogen oxides and volatile organic compounds undergo sunlight-driven reactions to generate ground-level ozone and other oxidants, which is why it is more common in sunny conditions. Ground-level ozone is never directly emitted, ruling out option B; options A and C describe particulate/condensation-based haze (closer to Delhi's winter smog), which is a physical accumulation process, not a photochemical one.

Tests atmospheric chemistry concept of primary vs secondary pollutants and photochemical reactions — related to UPSC's recurring questions on ground-level ozone and air pollutants.
