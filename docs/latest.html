Q18 · Science & Technology · Hard

An IISc-led study suggests strong internal magnetic fields could let some white dwarfs exceed the Chandrasekhar limit. With reference to astrophysics, which one of the following best describes the term 'Chandrasekhar limit'?

  1. It is the minimum mass a protostar must attain for hydrogen fusion to begin and a star to ignite.
  2. It is the maximum mass, about 1.4 times the Sun's mass, that a non-rotating, non-magnetised white dwarf can have while being supported against gravitational collapse by electron degeneracy pressure.
  3. It is the maximum mass beyond which a neutron star collapses into a black hole, also known as the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff limit.
  4. It is the minimum distance from a star within which a planet's atmosphere would be stripped away by stellar radiation.
Answer

(b) The Chandrasekhar limit (~1.4 solar masses) is the maximum mass an ordinary, non-rotating, non-magnetised white dwarf can sustain through electron degeneracy pressure before collapsing further; beyond it, the star would typically collapse into a neutron star or explode as a supernova. Option A describes the protostellar ignition mass, option C describes the distinct Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff limit for neutron stars, and option D is an unrelated, fabricated concept.
