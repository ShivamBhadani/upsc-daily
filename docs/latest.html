Q1 · Polity & Governance · Medium

The post-election wave of party-switching by MPs has renewed focus on India's anti-defection law. Consider the following statements regarding the Tenth Schedule of the Constitution:

  1. The Tenth Schedule was inserted into the Constitution by the 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985.
  2. A merger of a legislative party with another party is exempted from disqualification only if at least two-thirds of its members agree to the merger.
  3. A decision on disqualification of a Member of Parliament under the Tenth Schedule is taken by the President of India.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1, 2 and 3
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 2 only
  4. 1 and 3 only
Answer

(c) Statement 1 is correct — the Tenth Schedule was added by the 52nd Amendment, 1985. Statement 2 is correct — the two-thirds merger exemption was retained even after the 91st Amendment (2003) removed the one-third 'split' exemption. Statement 3 is incorrect — the Speaker (Lok Sabha) or Chairman (Rajya Sabha) decides disqualification petitions, not the President.

Tests the Tenth Schedule/anti-defection law (52nd Amendment, 1985) and the Speaker's adjudicatory role, a recurring Prelims theme.

Answer
