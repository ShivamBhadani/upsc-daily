Q13 · Geography · Medium

Delhi's air quality worsens every winter due to 'meteorological factors such as lower wind speeds and a drop in temperature,' which trap pollutants near the surface. Which one of the following best describes the atmospheric phenomenon primarily responsible for this trapping effect?

  1. The Coriolis effect, which slows down surface winds over northern India during the winter months
  2. The urban heat island effect, in which cities are significantly warmer than surrounding rural areas, drawing pollutants inward
  3. Orographic lifting caused by the Himalayas, which forces pollutant-laden air to rise and disperse over the plains
  4. Temperature inversion, in which a layer of warmer air sits above cooler surface air and prevents vertical mixing, trapping pollutants near the ground
Answer

(d) Temperature inversion is the correct mechanism: in winter, the ground cools faster than the air above it, creating a warm layer aloft that acts as a lid, preventing pollutants from dispersing upward — exactly the 'lower wind speed and drop in temperature' effect described. The Coriolis effect governs wind deflection due to Earth's rotation, not local calm conditions; the urban heat island effect concerns temperature differences between cities and their surroundings, not pollutant trapping; and orographic lifting would disperse, not trap, pollutants.

Tests the climatology concept of temperature inversion behind winter smog in the Indo-Gangetic Plain, a classic static-geography anchor for recurring Delhi pollution news.

The Supreme Court has referred its 2003 judgment upholding the two-child norm for contesting panchayat elections to a larger bench. Critically examine the constitutional and policy rationale for linking eligibility to contest local body elections with family size, and its implications for representation and population policy in India.

  • Right to contest elections is a statutory, not fundamental, right; panchayat election qualifications fall under Article 243F and the State List
  • Original rationale of population control versus the Court's observation that fertility-linked disqualification has 'lost relevance' amid demographic transition
  • Equity concerns: disproportionate impact on women, the poor and certain communities; conflict with reproductive autonomy under Article 21
  • Federalism angle: variation across states (e.g., Maharashtra, Haryana, Odisha) that have adopted or repealed such norms
