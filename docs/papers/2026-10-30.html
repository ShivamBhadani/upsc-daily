Q1 · Polity & Governance · Medium

The National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) has capped trade margins on anti-cancer medicines, prompting scrutiny of how drug prices are regulated in India. With reference to NPPA, which one of the following best describes its institutional status?

  1. It is an executive body set up by a government resolution, exercising price-control powers delegated under the Drugs (Prices Control) Order
  2. It is a statutory body created directly under the Essential Commodities Act, 1955 with independent rule-making powers over drug prices
  3. It is a constitutional authority established under Article 323B to adjudicate pharmaceutical pricing disputes
  4. It is a statutory regulator under the Ministry of Health, functioning similarly to the Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation
Answer

(a) NPPA was constituted in 1997 by a government resolution as an attached office under the Department of Pharmaceuticals, not by an Act of Parliament, so it is not statutory in itself. It exercises price-fixation powers through the Drugs (Prices Control) Order, which is issued under the Essential Commodities Act, 1955. There is no Article 323B tribunal for drug pricing, and NPPA sits under the Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers, not Health.
