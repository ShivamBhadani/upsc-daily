Q1 · Economy · Medium

Public Sector Banks (PSBs) are reportedly building Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) parameters into corporate credit risk assessment. In this context, consider the following statements about PSBs and ESG disclosure norms in India. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. A bank is classified as a 'Public Sector Bank' when the Government of India holds more than 50 percent of its equity capital.
  2. ESG disclosures for listed companies in India are mandated through an Integrated Reporting framework notified by the Reserve Bank of India.
  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer

(a) Statement 1 is correct: under the Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Acts, the Union Government must hold a majority (over 50%) stake for a bank to be a PSB. Statement 2 is wrong on two counts: mandatory ESG disclosure in India is done through the Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report (BRSR), not 'Integrated Reporting', and it is notified by SEBI (for listed companies), not the RBI.

Tests institutional ownership criteria of PSBs and the SEBI BRSR framework; similar to UPSC 2022 prelims questions on regulatory bodies governing financial disclosure.

Q2 · Environment & Ecology · Medium

A rare Royal Bengal Tiger sighting at Odisha's Debrigarh Wildlife Sanctuary has revived plans for tiger translocation there. Consider the following statements regarding the institutional and legal framework for tiger conservation in India. How many of the statements given above are correct?

  1. The National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA) is a statutory body constituted under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, as amended in 2006.
  2. Every notified tiger reserve is required to have a demarcated core (critical tiger habitat) area and a buffer area.
  3. Project Tiger was launched in 1973 and is administered by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs.
  4. The Wildlife Institute of India is a statutory regulatory authority empowered to grant mining and industrial clearances inside tiger habitats.
  1. Only one
  2. Only two
  3. Only three
  4. All four
Answer

(b) Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the NTCA was created by the 2006 amendment to the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, and the Act mandates demarcation of core and buffer areas for every tiger reserve. Statement 3 is wrong because Project Tiger is administered by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (via the NTCA), not the Ministry of Tribal Affairs. Statement 4 is wrong because the Wildlife Institute of India is an autonomous research and training institute, not a statutory clearance-granting authority.

Q3 · Science & Technology · Medium

Forest officials tracking the tiger that strayed into Debrigarh said its stripe pattern captured on camera traps would help confirm its source population. With reference to wildlife monitoring technology, which one of the following best describes this 'stripe-pattern identification' method?

  1. It is a technique of extracting mitochondrial DNA from pugmarks found in soil to trace a tiger's maternal genetic lineage.
  2. It is a photographic identification method that uses each tiger's unique and permanent stripe pattern, much like a human fingerprint, to distinguish one individual from another across camera-trap captures.
  3. It is a satellite-based thermal-imaging census method that counts tigers from aerial surveys without using ground-based camera traps.
  4. It is a whole-genome sequencing technique used to determine which tiger subspecies is present in a given forest patch.
Answer

(b) Stripe-pattern matching (the basis of tools like M-STrIPES used by India's tiger monitoring programme) works because each tiger's stripe pattern is unique and unchanging, allowing individuals to be told apart across camera-trap images over time and across landscapes. The other options describe genuinely different techniques (DNA pugmark analysis, aerial thermal census, genome sequencing) that are not what stripe-pattern identification involves.

Tests awareness of biometric/photographic identification technology used in India's All India Tiger Estimation; static syllabus link to wildlife census methodology.

Q4 · Polity & Governance · Hard

The Supreme Court took up the relocation of the Central Secretariat Library on its own motion after a newspaper report. In this context, consider the following two statements.

  1. Statement-I: In the Central Secretariat Library matter, the Supreme Court took suo motu cognizance of a newspaper report without any party filing a formal petition.
  2. Statement-II: The power of the Supreme Court to take suo motu cognizance of a matter is an express power explicitly enumerated in the text of Article 32 of the Constitution.
  1. Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
  2. Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct but Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
  3. Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
  4. Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct
Answer

(c) Statement-I is correct: the report describes the Court taking suo motu cognizance of a press report and directing the government to file an affidavit. Statement-II is incorrect because Article 32 guarantees the right to approach the Supreme Court for enforcement of fundamental rights by an aggrieved party; the suo motu power is not textually enumerated there but has evolved judicially, often invoked alongside Articles 136 and 142.

Tests the judicial (not textual) basis of suo motu cognizance, distinct from Article 32's writ jurisdiction; builds on UPSC's recurring Statement-I/II format on constitutional provisions since 2022.

Q8 · Polity & Governance · Hard

With reference to anti-conversion laws enacted by Indian states, consider the following statements:

  1. Statement-I: Several Indian states have enacted laws that place the burden of proof on a person facilitating a religious conversion to establish that it was voluntary.
  2. Statement-II: Article 25 of the Constitution guarantees an unqualified fundamental right to propagate one's religion, so such laws are constitutionally impermissible.
  1. Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
  2. Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct but Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
  3. Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
  4. Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct
Answer

(c) Maharashtra's anti-conversion law, effective from August 28, 2026, requires whoever facilitates a conversion to prove it was voluntary, confirming Statement-I. However, Article 25's right to propagate religion is not absolute; it is expressly subject to public order, morality, health, and other Part III provisions, and the Supreme Court (Rev. Stainislaus v. State of Madhya Pradesh, 1977) held that the right to propagate does not include a right to convert another person. So Statement-II is incorrect.

Tests Article 25 (freedom of conscience and free profession, practice and propagation of religion) and its reasonable restrictions; similar to UPSC's recurring fundamental rights/restrictions questions.

Q10 · Environment & Ecology · Medium

With reference to the global climate change regime under discussion at COP31 in Antalya, consider the following statements:

  1. COP31 is held under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC), which was adopted in 1992.
  2. Unlike the Paris Agreement, the Kyoto Protocol assigned legally binding emission reduction targets only to developed (Annex I) countries.
  3. The principle of 'Common but Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities' (CBDR-RC) obliges all parties, developed and developing alike, to undertake identical mitigation commitments.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3
Answer

(a) The UNFCCC was adopted at the 1992 Rio Earth Summit, making statement 1 correct. The Kyoto Protocol indeed imposed binding targets only on Annex I (developed) countries, unlike the Paris Agreement's universal NDC-based approach, making statement 2 correct. CBDR-RC does the opposite of statement 3: it differentiates obligations based on historical emissions and capability, not identical commitments, so statement 3 is incorrect.

Tests UNFCCC/Kyoto Protocol/Paris Agreement distinctions and the CBDR-RC principle, a frequently tested static climate-governance concept.

Q11 · Science & Technology · Hard

The scrutiny of advanced AI-chip transfers to China through third-country data centres has drawn attention to multilateral mechanisms governing dual-use technology exports. With reference to the 'Wassenaar Arrangement', which one of the following best describes it?

  1. A binding World Trade Organization agreement that mandates uniform tariff rates on semiconductor imports among member countries
  2. A multilateral export control regime under which participating states voluntarily coordinate controls on transfers of conventional arms and dual-use goods and technologies, including advanced semiconductors
  3. A bilateral treaty between the United States and China establishing permissible limits on artificial intelligence chip exports
  4. A United Nations Security Council sanctions mechanism targeting technology exports to specific proliferator states
Answer

(b) The Wassenaar Arrangement is a voluntary, non-binding multilateral regime in which member states coordinate (not mandate via treaty) export controls on conventional arms and dual-use goods/technologies, which is the backdrop to controversies like restricted AI chips reaching China via third countries. It is not a WTO tariff instrument, not a bilateral US-China treaty, and not a UNSC sanctions mechanism.
