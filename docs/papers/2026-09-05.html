With reference to the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls conducted by the Election Commission of India, consider the following statements. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. The Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls is conducted by the Election Commission of India under powers derived from the Representation of the People Act, 1950.

  3. A person whose name is deleted from the draft electoral roll during SIR has no legal remedy and must wait for the next roll revision cycle to be re-included.

  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3
Answer

Statement 3 is wrong because EC guidelines explicitly allow an aggrieved elector to file a claim/objection against deletion during the draft-roll stage itself, not just at the next cycle.

Q8 · Economy · Medium

With reference to government bonds, consider the following statements. Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

  1. Statement-I: A sell-off in government bonds is generally accompanied by a rise in bond yields.
  2. Statement-II: The price of a bond and its yield are inversely related, so a fall in bond prices raises the effective yield to a new buyer.
  1. Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
  2. Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct but Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
  3. Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
  4. Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct
Answer

(a) Bond price and yield move inversely because a bond's coupon is fixed; when investors sell, prices fall, and the fixed coupon then represents a higher percentage return (yield) on the lower price. This inverse relationship is precisely why a sell-off raises yields, so Statement-II correctly explains Statement-I.

Tests the bond price-yield relationship, a recurring static concept behind UPSC's monetary policy and G-Sec questions (cf. UPSC 2021 RBI expansionary policy question).

Q13 · Geography · Medium

With reference to map projections, and in the context of the UN resolution favouring the Equal Earth projection over the Mercator projection, which one of the following best explains why the Mercator projection makes Greenland appear roughly the same size as Africa?

  1. The Mercator projection is an equal-area projection that preserves true relative size everywhere at the cost of shape.
  2. The Mercator projection is a conformal cylindrical projection that preserves angles and shapes for navigation but progressively inflates the area of landmasses farther from the equator.
  3. The Mercator projection is centred on the Prime Meridian, which distorts longitudinal distances near the poles.
  4. The Mercator projection is an azimuthal projection that distorts distances only along great-circle routes.
Answer

(b) The Mercator projection is a conformal cylindrical projection, valued for preserving angles/shapes for navigation, but this comes at the cost of increasingly exaggerating the area of regions away from the equator (like Greenland) relative to equatorial regions (like Africa). It is not equal-area (option A), its distortion is latitude-driven, not centred on a meridian (option C), and it is cylindrical, not azimuthal (option D).

Tests properties of map projections (conformal/equal-area/azimuthal), a standard static geography syllabus topic.
