With reference to the taxation of listed equity shares in India, consider the following statements. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. Gains from the sale of listed equity shares are treated as long-term capital gains only if the shares were held for more than 12 months before sale.
  2. Securities Transaction Tax (STT) is applicable only on transactions in securities executed through a recognised stock exchange, and not on off-market transactions.
  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer

(c) Listed equity shares are classified long-term only beyond a 12-month holding period (short-term below that), so statement 1 is correct. STT applies only to transactions routed through a recognised stock exchange (or stock exchange-cleared derivatives), not to off-market/private transfers, so statement 2 is also correct.

Q2 · Economy · Medium

Consider the following two statements regarding the Reserve Bank of India's recent measures to deal with rupee volatility.

  1. Statement-I: The RBI's recent measures, such as a dedicated dollar window for oil marketing companies, are aimed at curbing volatility in the rupee's exchange rate rather than defending a fixed value for the currency.
  2. Statement-II: Under India's current exchange rate arrangement, the rupee's external value is market-determined, and the RBI intervenes mainly to smoothen excessive short-term volatility rather than to fix a target rate.
  1. Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
  2. Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct but Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
  3. Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
  4. Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct
Answer

(a) India follows a managed float exchange rate regime in which the rupee's value is largely market-determined, and the RBI's mandate is to curb excessive volatility, not to peg the currency at a fixed level — this is exactly what the Finance Minister clarified. Statement-II states this regime rationale, which directly explains why Statement-I's measures target volatility rather than a fixed value.

Q5 · International Relations · Medium

Consider the following statements about the International Monetary Fund (IMF). How many of the above statements are correct?

  1. The IMF publishes the World Economic Outlook report to assess global macroeconomic prospects.
  2. A member country's quota in the IMF determines both its voting power and its financial contribution to the Fund.
  3. The Special Drawing Right (SDR) issued by the IMF is a freely tradable currency that can be used directly for commercial transactions.

Answer

Q7 · Environment & Ecology · Easy

With reference to hydrogen energy, which one of the following best describes the term 'green hydrogen'?

  1. Hydrogen produced through steam methane reforming of natural gas without carbon capture, commonly used in fertiliser production.
  2. Hydrogen produced through steam methane reforming of natural gas combined with carbon capture and storage technology.
  3. Hydrogen produced as a by-product of the chlor-alkali industry, generally used captively within the same facility.
  4. Hydrogen produced through electrolysis of water using electricity generated from renewable energy sources.
Answer

(d) Green hydrogen is specifically hydrogen produced by electrolysing water using renewable electricity (solar, wind, hydro), resulting in near-zero carbon emissions — this is the pathway NTPC is pursuing for its green ammonia/methanol hub. Steam methane reforming without carbon capture yields 'grey' hydrogen, the same process with carbon capture yields 'blue' hydrogen, and chlor-alkali by-product hydrogen is a separate, fossil-linked category, so these are near-miss distractors.

Q13 · Economy · Medium

The recent public dispute over satellite internet access in India has drawn attention to the government's policy on spectrum for satellite communication services. With reference to this policy, which one of the following best describes the approach India has adopted?

  1. Satellite spectrum is allocated through competitive auction in the same manner as terrestrial mobile spectrum, ensuring identical entry cost for all operators.
  2. Satellite spectrum is allocated administratively on a shared, non-exclusive basis, with charges fixed by the government rather than determined through auction.
  3. Satellite spectrum allocation is an exclusive prerogative of State governments exercised under the Indian Telegraph Act, 1885.
  4. Satellite spectrum is reserved exclusively for public sector undertakings, with private operators barred from access.
Answer

(b) India has adopted administrative (not auction-based) allocation of satellite spectrum, since it is inherently shared and non-exclusive across orbital users, with the government fixing charges — a key point of contention raised in the Starlink-Reliance Jio dispute. Auctioning (option a) applies to terrestrial spectrum like 4G/5G bands, not satellite spectrum. Options c and d misstate the allocating authority and wrongly exclude private players, who are in fact licensed to operate satellite services.

Tests spectrum allocation methods (administrative vs auction), a recurring Economy/infrastructure theme since the 2012 Presidential Reference on natural resource allocation.
