Q1 · Economy · Medium

The Union Cabinet's clearance for sale of a majority stake in IDBI Bank to Fairfax Financial Holdings has been described as the biggest privatization since Air India. In this context, consider the following statements:

  1. The Department of Investment and Public Asset Management (DIPAM), under the Ministry of Finance, oversees the Central Government's disinvestment process.
  2. IDBI Bank was reclassified by the RBI as a private sector bank after the Life Insurance Corporation of India acquired majority ownership in it.
  3. The Tata Group's acquisition of Air India, completed in 2022, was India's first strategic disinvestment of a public sector bank.
  4. Strategic disinvestment involves the government selling a stake along with transfer of management control to a private buyer.

How many of the above statements are correct?

  1. Only one
  2. Only two
  3. Only three
  4. All four
Answer

(c) Statements 1, 2 and 4 are correct: DIPAM handles disinvestment, IDBI Bank was reclassified as private after LIC's 2019 majority stake acquisition, and strategic disinvestment by definition includes transfer of management control. Statement 3 is wrong because Air India is an airline, not a bank, so the claim misdescribes the category of asset.

Tests strategic disinvestment policy and DIPAM's role, similar to UPSC 2020 prelims question on disinvestment and strategic sale.

Q2 · Polity & Governance · Medium

Following the denotification of Jantar Mantar as a designated protest site and the MHA's directions to States on preventing mobilisation of protesters, consider the following statements regarding the right to peaceful assembly in India:

  1. Article 19(1)(b) of the Constitution guarantees citizens the right to assemble peaceably and without arms.
  2. This right can be exercised with arms if prior police permission has been obtained.
  3. Reasonable restrictions on this right can be imposed only through a constitutional amendment, not by ordinary legislation.
  4. Public order, and the sovereignty and integrity of India, are grounds on which the right to assembly may be reasonably restricted.

How many of the above statements are correct?

  1. Only one
  2. Only two
  3. Only three
  4. All four
Answer

(b) Statements 1 and 4 are correct: Article 19(1)(b) guarantees peaceable, unarmed assembly, and Article 19(3) permits reasonable restrictions on grounds including public order and sovereignty/integrity of India. Statement 2 is false since the right explicitly excludes assembly with arms, and statement 3 is false because restrictions are imposed through ordinary law (e.g., Section 163 BNSS), not constitutional amendment.

Tests Article 19(1)(b) and 19(3) reasonable restrictions, akin to UPSC questions on fundamental rights and their limitations.

Q7 · Social Issues & Schemes · Hard

A District Collector in Chittoor called for collective efforts to eradicate child marriages. In this context, consider the following statements:

  1. Statement-I: The legal minimum age of marriage for women in India under the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006 is 18 years.
  2. Statement-II: Under the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006, all child marriages are automatically void, regardless of whether either party exercises the option to annul it.
  1. Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
  2. Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct but Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
  3. Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
  4. Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct
Answer

(c) Statement-I is correct: the legal age of marriage for women is 18 years under the Act. Statement-II is incorrect because the Act makes child marriages voidable at the option of the child party (Section 3), not automatically void; only marriages procured through force, deceit, trafficking or enticement are declared void under Section 12.

Tests the voidable-versus-void distinction under the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006, a recurring nuance in UPSC's social legislation questions.

Q8 · Polity & Governance · Medium

With reference to the revision of electoral rolls in India, consider the following statements:

  1. Article 324 of the Constitution vests the Election Commission with the power of superintendence, direction and control of elections, including the preparation of electoral rolls.
  2. The Special Summary Revision (SSR) is a one-time exercise conducted only in years when general elections are due, whereas the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is carried out every year.
  3. Under the Representation of the People Act, 1950, electoral rolls must be revised before every general election to the House of the People or a State Legislative Assembly.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3
Answer

(c) Statements 1 and 3 are correct: Article 324 gives the EC overall authority over elections including rolls, and Section 21(2) of the RPA, 1950 mandates revision before every general election. Statement 2 reverses the facts — the SSR is the routine exercise normally conducted annually with January 1 as the qualifying date, while the SIR is the special, more intensive one-time exercise; the EC in fact skipped the SSR in 2026.

Tests Article 324 and the statutory basis of electoral roll revision under the RPA, 1950 — similar to UPSC questions on powers and functions of the Election Commission.

Consider the following statements regarding caste-based reservation in India:

  1. Article 16(4) guarantees a fundamental right to reservation that is enforceable by courts through a writ of mandamus.
  2. The Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order, 1950 restricts Scheduled Caste status to persons professing the Hindu, Sikh or Buddhist religion.
  3. The Supreme Court has held that backwardness associated with a person's pre-conversion caste status can be carried forward to claim reservation benefits in the religion to which the person converts.
  4. The Mandal Commission recommended reservation for Other Backward Classes based on social and educational backwardness.

How many of the above statements are correct?

  1. Only one
  2. Only two
  3. Only three
  4. All four
Answer

(b) Statements 2 and 4 are correct: the Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order, 1950 confines SC status to Hindus, Sikhs and Buddhists, and the Mandal Commission based OBC reservation on social and educational backwardness. Statement 1 is wrong — the Supreme Court in Indra Sawhney held Article 16(4) is an enabling provision, not a fundamental right enforceable by mandamus. Statement 3 is wrong because the Supreme Court recently held the opposite — backwardness from a pre-conversion status cannot be carried forward into the religion converted to.
